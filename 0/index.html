Becoming Friends with Your Camera

Three small experiments on how perspective depends on where the camera is, not on how much it zooms.

Susanna Atanessian · Fall 2026

1 · Selfie 2 · Architecture 3 · Dolly Zoom

01Selfie: The Wrong Way vs. The Right Way

A portrait taken from close up, next to the same portrait taken from several feet back with the camera zoomed in so the face is the same size.

Why it happens. Perspective is set by the center of projection, meaning where the camera physically is. Up close, the distance from the lens to the nose is much smaller than the distance to the ears, so the nose projects much larger. Stepping back makes those distances nearly equal, and zooming just scales the whole image back up without changing the relative sizes.

02Architectural Perspective Compression

The same idea in reverse. A building photographed from far away with zoom, then again after walking up to it with no zoom, keeping the building the same size in the frame.

Why it happens. From far away, the ratio of distances between the near and far parts of the scene is close to 1, so they project at similar sizes and depth is compressed. Up close, that ratio is large, so near things balloon and the scene looks deep. A long focal length is often blamed for "compression", but the real cause is the distance the long lens lets you stand at.

03The Dolly Zoom

Hitchcock's "Vertigo" shot. The camera moves backward while zooming in, so the subject stays the same size while the background appears to expand behind it.

Why it happens. Zooming keeps the subject's size fixed as the camera pulls back, but the change in camera position changes the perspective. The background, which is much farther away, shrinks less than the subject would have, so relative to the subject it appears to grow and rush toward the viewer.
